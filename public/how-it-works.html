How MockMentor works

A realistic mock interview built around your actual resume and the exact job you're targeting — not a generic question bank.

Most interview prep tools hand you the same list of questions everyone else gets. Real interviewers don't work that way. They read your resume, they read the job description, and they ask the questions that sit right on the line between what you've done and what the role needs. MockMentor does the same thing, then coaches you afterward.

1. You give it two things

Upload your resume as a PDF or paste it as text, and paste the full job description for the role you're interviewing for. That's the whole setup — no account, no forms, no wizard. The more complete the job description, the sharper the interview, so paste the entire posting rather than just the title.

2. It figures out where you're exposed

Before asking anything, MockMentor reads both documents and works out three things about you — your relevant experience, your concrete skills, and your gaps relative to this specific job — and three things about the role: its hard requirements, the competencies a real interviewer would probe, and the red flags they'd be listening for. You see this analysis on a review screen before the interview starts, so you know exactly what's coming.

3. It runs a real interview, one question at a time

MockMentor generates 8–10 questions tailored to the role, mixing behavioral ("tell me about a time…"), technical, and situational ("how would you handle…") types based on what the job actually calls for. Questions arrive one at a time, and it waits for your answer before moving on — just like a real conversation. You can type your answers or speak them with the microphone; text is always available if speech recognition acts up.

4. It pushes back when an answer is thin

This is where it stops feeling like a form and starts feeling like an interview. When an answer is vague, hypothetical, or dodges the question, MockMentor asks a realistic follow-up — "Can you give me a specific example?", "What was your role in that, specifically?", "What was the measurable outcome?" — before moving on. That pressure is the point: it's far better to fumble here than in the room that counts.

5. It gives you honest, question-by-question feedback

At the end you get a structured debrief: for every question, what worked, what fell short (and what the interviewer was actually listening for), and a concrete example of a stronger answer built from your real background — never invented achievements. Plus an overall read and the two-to-four highest-leverage things to fix before the real thing.

The tone

Throughout, MockMentor behaves like a senior hiring manager who genuinely wants you to get the offer: warm and encouraging, but honest enough to tell you when an answer won't land. That combination — supportive but not flattering — is what makes the practice actually useful.
